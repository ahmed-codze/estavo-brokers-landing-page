Research case study: comparing developer offers across six versions

By Estavo Brokers · Published 2 October 2026 · Research case study

Comparing six versions of Tiba Golden inventory produced 188 provisional recurring-offer groups. Changes in area, view and status showed why a repeated unit identifier is insufficient proof of an unchanged property or a completed sale.

The question behind the study

Can older developer price books help a broker assess a historical quotation? Our 30 September 2026 collection examined public developer download and media catalogues, including documents no longer highlighted on the current downloads page. The question was about evidence quality, not whether a specific project guarantees returns.

This is an independent research case study of public-source records. It is not a customer testimonial, an endorsement or a claim of partnership with the source publisher.

Preserve versions before comparing them

Red Sea Way’s public media catalogue exposed older Tiba Golden documents. We retained the source bytes and hashes and matched recurring entries using publisher project, floor and unit identifier. Filename dates, upload timestamps and document dates were preserved as different evidence classes.

Spreadsheet creation dates described file ancestry, not the date of each price. Hidden rows and cached formula values were retained during private analysis; formulas were not recalculated into invented offers.

These flags overlap: do not add them to calculate a total number of affected properties. Even stable area and type do not prove unchanged finishing, quality or physical identity.

Why a sold status is not a sale price

The broader spreadsheet inventory channel contained 475 hidden rows and 461 rows marked Sold or Sold out. These are counts across inventory snapshots, not confirmed unique property sales; the categories can overlap. A stored list price next to a sold marker does not reveal the negotiated price or completion date.

Payment basis, discounts and unit-state changes also need review before interpreting a difference between two quotations. The collection did not create realized-return labels.

A broker checklist for historical developer offers

  1. Match the exact project, phase, building, floor and unit reference.
  2. Check whether area, type, view, finishing or status changed.
  3. Record what the stated date actually means.
  4. Separate cash quotations, instalment quotations and currency equivalents.
  5. Verify whether an apparent sale has a transaction record rather than only a status flag.
  6. For current availability, obtain a fresh booking-source confirmation.

What can be published responsibly

We publish aggregate findings, a source-version ledger and original methodological analysis. The raw spreadsheets, hidden rows and individual offers are not republished here. Source redistribution and training rights remain unverified; public accessibility is not a commercial data licence.

The research demonstrates a review method. It does not establish national property growth, investment performance or that these units are available today.

Questions brokers ask

Are recurring offer groups repeat sales?

No. They are provisional matches between publisher references across documents, awaiting identity and economic-term review.

Can I add the changed-area, view and status counts?

No. The flags overlap, and a group can have more than one kind of change.
