How to create a property PDF offer clients can actually use

By Estavo Brokers · Published 2 October 2026 · Practical guide

A useful property PDF offer answers why the option fits, what it costs, what is included, when it is due and what to do next. Use verified details and an obvious contact route; review the document on a phone before sending it.

Open with the client’s brief and the reason for the option

Start with a short description of the confirmed need and why this property belongs on the shortlist. Avoid generic “best opportunity” language. If a unit compromises on area, location or delivery timing, say so. A clear explanation helps the buyer understand why you selected it.

Use this proposal structure

  1. Your name, contact information and proposal date.
  2. Client need and reason for the recommendation.
  3. Developer, project, phase and exact unit or reference model.
  4. Location, area definition, type, rooms, floor, view and finishing.
  5. Quoted price, currency, payment schedule and extra charges.
  6. Delivery information and availability verification status.
  7. Source/check date, missing terms and validity if supplied.
  8. Next action: confirm details, discuss a comparison or arrange a viewing.

Include only images you are permitted to use. A reference-layout image should be labeled as a reference, not represented as a photograph of the exact unit.

Keep unconfirmed information visible

Use “not supplied” or “awaiting confirmation” for unknown charges, final booking status or unclear delivery terms. Do not fill the gap with a nearby project’s details. Separate a quoted offer from an accepted reservation and keep the price basis clear: cash and instalment quotations may differ.

Write a short cover message

“I have shortlisted this option because it fits [confirmed requirement]. The PDF shows the specifications and supplied payment terms. [Item] still needs confirmation before booking. Would you prefer to discuss the comparison or arrange a viewing?”

Replace the placeholders with reviewed information. This template is illustrative and is not a customer testimonial or a sales result.

Check the PDF on a phone

  • Can the recipient identify the project and price without zooming repeatedly?
  • Are table labels and currency visible?
  • Do Arabic words, numerals and contact details display correctly?
  • Are images legible and correctly labeled?
  • Is the file name descriptive rather than “final-final”?
  • Are the contact link and next action easy to find?

Open the exported document, not just the editor preview. When comparing alternatives, make sure identical labels mean identical things in both offers.

Keep a reviewed version and learn from questions

Save the sent version and its check date. If the source changes a price or term, prepare a new dated version and explain the change to the client. Do not silently overwrite an offer the buyer is already discussing.

Review recurring questions after sending proposals. If clients repeatedly ask about initial cash or handover, make those sections clearer. Evaluate your own response and viewing data before claiming a proposal design improved sales.

Questions brokers ask

Is the PDF a booking confirmation?

No. A proposal summarizes supplied information. Reservation status and final contractual terms require separate confirmation.

Should I include a missing fee as zero?

No. Label it as not supplied or awaiting confirmation; zero is a factual amount, not a placeholder.
