Research case study: verifying 297 archived property-price snapshots

By Estavo Brokers · Published 2 October 2026 · Research case study

Our 30 September 2026 research recovered 297 archive-verified asking-price snapshots across 212 portal listing IDs. Of those snapshots, 251 included a usable positive area. The result improves historical evidence coverage; it does not establish completed-sale prices or investment returns.

The research problem

Historical property claims often rely on an old screenshot, an undated spreadsheet or today’s description of what prices used to be. Individual archived listing pages can also fail to replay. We investigated whether archived search/category pages and old listing formats could preserve more usable evidence.

This is a research case study, not a customer success story. No broker sales uplift, time saving or transaction outcome was measured.

How the evidence was collected

The acquisition targeted archived Property Finder category pages and Aqarmap’s older listing URL format. Search cards retained prices and areas; some older Aqarmap pages retained structured listing data. Original source bytes, retrieval metadata, extraction locators and hashes were saved privately.

A replay was accepted as archive-verified only when its response capture metadata agreed with the effective Wayback replay timestamp. The archive capture date and any advertiser posting-date claim remained separate fields. Neither was automatically treated as the date of a sale or of an effective price.

All 297 snapshots stated currency; 251 also had a usable positive area. There were 74 groups where a listing ID appeared more than once. None of those groups showed a changed asking price in this collection. A repeated listing ID is not independently verified physical-property identity.

What we rejected

Rediscovered datasets identical to earlier originals added zero new rows. Missing areas were not filled with guessed values, and execution timestamps were not turned into historical price dates. Failed downloads and empty discovery results were recorded as attempts rather than counted as observations.

Exact snapshot overlap checks found no duplicates with the earlier normalized collections. Different advertisements can still describe the same property, so that is not proof that all physical properties are unique.

What a broker can use from the method

When discussing a historical quote, keep the original source, the exact observed price and currency, the area if stated and the date type. Explain whether it is an asking price, a market benchmark or a verified transaction. Do not turn a repeated old advertisement into a return calculation.

The observed archive coverage is highly uneven across years and is not representative of Egyptian housing. Use the method to improve evidence discipline, not to claim that this sample proves a national trend.

Questions brokers ask

Did the research prove historical property returns?

No. It recovered archived asking-price observations, not verified repeat transactions.

Are 297 snapshots the same as 297 unique properties?

No. They span 212 portal listing IDs, and physical-property identity was not independently verified.
