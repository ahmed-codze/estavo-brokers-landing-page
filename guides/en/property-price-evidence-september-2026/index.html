Egypt property-price evidence report — September 2026

By Estavo Brokers · Published 2 October 2026 · Market evidence report

Our bounded September 2026 research added 2,268 records, but these are a mix of asking-price snapshots, developer quotations and market benchmarks. The main finding is an evidence-quality gap: more records do not automatically establish sale prices, current inventory or a reliable national price trend.

Scope and method

Collected on 30 September 2026 and published on 2 October 2026, this report analyzes one additional evidence collection. It excludes totals from two earlier collections. Selection followed recoverable archived pages and public developer source catalogues; it was not a random or representative sample of Egyptian housing.

The archive channel contains 346 records; the developer-source channel contains 1,922 records across seven projects. Sources were retained with hashes and exact extraction references. The public release contains aggregate analysis and representative source pointers, not raw listings, customer details or a redistributed price database.

Finding: archive coverage is uneven

The 297 archived asking-price snapshots include 26 from 2012, 32 from 2013 and 239 from 2014. Thus 80.5% of these archive snapshots are from 2014 (239 ÷ 297, rounded to one decimal). Only 251 snapshots contain both explicit currency and usable positive area: 84.5% (251 ÷ 297).

That concentration reflects what this acquisition recovered, not where buyers were most active or how national prices moved. Averages across these years would be vulnerable to changing location and property mix. Forty-nine report-level benchmarks remain separate from individual-unit observations.

Finding: document dates are not economic price dates

The developer channel has 1,310 records with 2019–2023 document-date evidence and 612 current-source observations from 2026. Upload timestamps, filename dates, API update times and page publication dates have different meanings. None was automatically assigned as an effective economic price date.

All 2,268 economic-date fields remained unassigned in this research export, and all training-approval flags remained false. This describes the evidence-review state, not a claim that every source lacks any date. More interpretation is needed before these records can support comparable historical price labels.

Finding: recurring references need identity review

Across six Tiba Golden versions, 188 provisional recurring-offer groups were identified. Three changed reported area, 37 changed view and 56 changed status; these flags overlap. The archive channel also has 74 repeated listing-ID groups, none showing a changed asking price in this collection.

Neither result establishes verified repeat sales. Stable identifiers can coexist with changed specification, reused marketing references or stale offers. Likewise, sold-status workbook rows do not establish achieved transaction prices.

What brokers should do with these findings

  1. Ask whether a number is an asking price, cash quote, instalment quote, benchmark or verified transaction.
  2. Record source and date type, not just the downloaded date.
  3. Compare location, property type, area definition, finishing and payment basis.
  4. Keep unconfirmed availability and charges explicit.
  5. Before booking, get current written terms from the responsible source.

Limits and citation

This is an original market-evidence audit, not a national price index, valuation or return forecast. Asking prices are not achieved prices. Rows are not unique properties. The developer sample is concentrated in recoverable resort-project sources, and the archives are uneven by year. No national growth rate, return projection or prediction-accuracy claim is supported.

Questions brokers ask

Does this report show how much Egyptian property prices rose?

No. The sample is not representative, economic price dates are unresolved and asking prices are not verified transactions.

Can I use the record count as available stock?

No. It includes historical, sold-status, benchmark and model-reference records. Current availability requires a separate check.
