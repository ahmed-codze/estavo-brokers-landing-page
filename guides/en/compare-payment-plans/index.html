How to compare property payment plans without misleading a client

By Estavo Brokers · Published 2 October 2026 · Practical guide

Compare payment plans using the same unit specification and a dated schedule of all required payments. Show the initial cash, near-term commitments, total quoted obligation and extra charges separately. A longer plan or smaller percentage down payment is not automatically more affordable.

Start with comparable units and dated quotations

Before discussing percentages, align unit type, area definition, finishing, phase, delivery basis and currency. A cash quote and an instalment quote are different offers. State which basis applies and when it was checked. If either quotation is missing a material term, ask the source before ranking it.

Write out the schedule instead of quoting the headline

  • Booking amount and whether it forms part of the down payment.
  • Down payment and each additional milestone.
  • Number, frequency and amount of recurring instalments.
  • Handover payment and any balloon payment.
  • Maintenance, parking, club or other charges, with payment timing.
  • Any stated cash discount and its conditions.

Use the source’s actual schedule. Do not divide the remaining balance equally if the contract uses stepped or irregular payments.

Show the arithmetic with an explicitly illustrative example

Suppose a fictional offer has a quoted price of EGP 5,000,000, a 10% down payment and the remaining price divided into 20 equal quarterly instalments, with no other price payments. The down payment is EGP 500,000; the remaining EGP 4,500,000 produces EGP 225,000 per quarter.

This example is arithmetic only, not live inventory or developer terms. Additional charges, different schedules and discounts change the result. Twenty quarterly instalments do not mean the developer also offers a monthly payment option.

Test the schedule against the buyer’s cash timing

Ask whether the client can meet each payment at its actual due date. Show the cumulative amount due over the first year, then any later large milestones. A buyer who can afford an average monthly equivalent may still be unable to meet a large quarterly or handover payment.

Keep unconfirmed charges visible as questions. A plan with missing fees should not be presented as cheaper simply because fewer items were supplied.

Use a comparison prompt that preserves unknowns

“Compare these two quoted schedules. List booking cash, down payment, dated instalments, handover amounts, extra charges and total stated payments. Identify missing terms and whether the specifications differ. Use only the supplied figures; do not assume a discount, equal instalments or a guaranteed return.”

Estavo’s broker AI can help explain available payment-plan information. Check the underlying terms and arithmetic before sending the comparison. This guide does not depend on a separate Estavo calculator.

Present the decision in the client’s language

Explain which plan fits the client’s confirmed cash timeline, which requires more near-term cash and what remains unknown. Keep that suitability judgement separate from forecasts about investment returns. Before booking, obtain the current written terms from the responsible source and put the verified schedule into the proposal.

Questions brokers ask

Is the lowest down payment the best plan?

Not necessarily. Later milestones, extra charges, total price and delivery timing may make it unsuitable for the client.

Can I turn a quarterly instalment into a monthly obligation?

You may explain a monthly budgeting equivalent, but label it clearly. It does not change the developer’s actual due dates or contract.
